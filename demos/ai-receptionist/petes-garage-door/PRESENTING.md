# Presenting the Pete's Garage Door Repair demo

Emergency repair runs on one number, and Pete is often under a door on another job. The demo
shows Ava picking up those calls, flagging the urgent ones, and texting Pete the details.
Every answer has a one-tap button, so either call clicks through in about a minute.

## Call 1: Broken spring, car trapped (the urgent case)

1. Tap **Call (519) 914-3471**. Ava says Pete's out on a job and she's taking his calls.
2. Tap **My garage door spring broke and my car's stuck inside. I need to get to work.**
   Ava picks up the broken spring and the trapped car from that one sentence, marks the call
   **URGENT**, and tells the caller not to touch the door and to keep people and pets clear.
3. Tap **No one is hurt** and the sample name, address and number, then **Yes, that's right**.
4. Pete's text: red **URGENT - Broken spring, car trapped** banner, **Priority: URGENT**, and
   **Next step: Pete calls back as soon as he's clear of the current job**.

## Call 2: Noisy, slow door (the routine case)

1. Start over, pick **Noisy, slow door**, then Call.
2. Tap through the sample answers.
3. The text has no banner: **Priority: Routine**, **Next step: Pete calls back to book a time**.

## Worth showing: the safety rule

Tap **Can I just open it myself?**, or type *"how do I release the spring?"*. Ava never gives
DIY advice: she says not to touch it, that springs and cables can seriously hurt someone, to
leave the door as it is and keep people and pets clear, and that Pete will handle it. Then
she goes back to her question. This is the part most owners care about.

Also: *"How much will it cost?"* and *"When can Pete get here?"* get no price and no time, and
*"Am I talking to a real person?"* gets "I'm an AI receptionist for Pete's Garage Door Repair..."

## What counts as URGENT

A broken spring or cable (only with breakage words: broke, snapped, cracked, came apart), a
loud bang or the door slamming or dropping, door off its tracks, a door that won't close (home
left open), a car trapped inside, or anyone hurt or stuck. Noise, slowness, remotes, keypads and
servicing are routine.

Questions never escalate a call. *"How much does a new spring cost?"* gets the price line and
Ava goes back to her question; *"Do you sell springs?"* or *"How long do springs last?"* get
"That's a good question for Pete." Only what the caller states about the door sets the priority.
